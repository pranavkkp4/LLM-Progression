\begin{abstract}
\noindent
Between October 2018 and July 2026, language models moved from BERT, a
340-million-parameter encoder that could barely generate text, to
trillion-parameter agentic systems that solve International Mathematical
Olympiad problems, repair real software repositories, and operate desktop
computers. This paper reviews that progression, quantifies it, and tests three
practical claims with code. First, using public benchmark series (GLUE,
SuperGLUE, MMLU, SWE-bench Verified), we estimate that agentic-coding
capability, measured as the odds of solving a real GitHub issue, has grown by a
factor of ${\sim}5.8\times$ per year since October 2024. Second, we document the
collapse of the capability--cost curve: GPT-5.6 Luna, OpenAI's budget tier at
\$0.20/\$1.20 per million input/output tokens, matches or beats GPT-5.5 ---
the flagship of eleven weeks earlier --- on most agentic and professional-work
benchmarks in the vendor's own tables, and exceeds the August 2025 flagship
GPT-5's SWE-bench Verified score by ${\sim}18$ points on an independent
harness's reconstruction. Third, we show that
the frontier has fragmented by task: Kimi~K3 leads human-preference frontend
coding, Claude Fable~5 leads repository-level coding, GPT-5.6 Sol leads agentic
terminal work, and Claude Opus~5 quadrupled the record on the ARC-AGI-3
novel-reasoning benchmark. An oracle that routes each task to the best of six
public models recovers the full per-benchmark optimum, and we show a two-model
router already captures all of that gain. Finally, as a proof of method, we run
a live experiment: self-consistency sampling lifts a 0.5-billion-parameter open
model's accuracy on grade-school math problems substantially over greedy
decoding, demonstrating that test-time compute remains an underused lever for
improving models without retraining. All data, code, unit tests, and
compilation scripts are provided.
\end{abstract}
